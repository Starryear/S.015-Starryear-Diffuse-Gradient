# S.015 · Starryear Diffuse Gradient

Transform any user photograph into one complete vertical 9:16 artwork: an untouched 16:9 photographic evidence band at the very top, followed by a larger night-toned diffuse-gradient surreal reconstruction of the same theme.

## Fixed structure and evidence

Use 1152×2048 or an exact proportional resolution. At width 1152, the top band is 648 pixels high and the lower panel is 1400 pixels high. Both span the full width. No border, margin, title, logo, or watermark. A clean join or subtle color response below the seam is allowed; generated content must never cover the evidence.

Only proportional resizing and 16:9 cropping are permitted for the original. Never recolor, filter, retouch, extend, inpaint, erase, or add content. Protect faces, subjects, hands, landmarks, and meaningful relationships when selecting the crop; shift the crop when centering would damage them. Generate the lower panel separately, then deterministically embed the actual source file. A convincing AI reconstruction cannot substitute for original evidence.

## Understand the photograph

Identify internally: its theme beyond object names; the minimum features preserving identity, species, structure, spatial meaning, or function; its dominant direction, scale relationships, visual center, light/dark rhythm, and 3–5 source colors; and which relationships must remain versus which forms may enlarge, float, repeat, dissolve, penetrate, or transform.

## Reconstruct rather than copy

Preserve theme, emotion, and essential identity anchors while redesigning composition, scale, space, and material. Do not merely blur, recolor, or repaint the source. Choose one source motif for a substantial but traceable transformation: visibly different at first glance, recognizably related at second glance.

Choose one main surreal proposition and at most one supporting mechanism:

- Abnormal scale: a source element becomes a huge environment or miniature world.
- Weightlessness: subjects, fragments, or shadows form a rhythm beyond gravity.
- Material change: matter becomes light mist, translucent membranes, liquid gradients, or airborne color.
- Spatial reversal: inside/outside, near/far, up/down, or container/content exchange roles in a coherent impossibility.
- Temporal echoes: one source form reappears at varied opacity, scale, and depth to suggest movement or memory, not simple duplication.
- Environment generation: part of the subject becomes terrain, sky, light bands, or spatial structure.

Do not add unrelated spaceships, planets, portals, eyes, giant figures, or generic surreal props. The unfamiliar must grow from this photograph.

## Gradient-first composition

Diffuse gradient here does not mean a night photograph, smoke, shallow focus, or realism with Bloom. Large freeform gradient fields overlap and mix without hard edges; objects are recognition anchors embedded in those fields. The viewer must see color relationships and light flow before recognizing the subject.

- Smooth low-detail fields dominate at least 65% of the lower panel; recognizable objects and clear texture together occupy no more than about 35%. Target 65–80% fields and 20–35% simplified anchors. Do not render a full realistic scene and overlay fog.
- Use 3–5 asymmetrical, differently sized, partially overlapping freeform fields. At least one enters from outside the canvas. At least one crosses an anchor or visibly mixes a third intermediate color with another field. Avoid uniform horizontal, vertical, or concentric gradients.
- Every field has a colored core, extremely broad transparent falloff, and fully disappearing edges. Visible circular spots, elliptical patches, airbrush rings, lens halos, and hard bands fail.
- One main field establishes the focal center; 1–2 secondary fields supply direction and warm/cool relationships; remaining fields provide dim support. Do not make equal-size, equal-brightness glowing blobs.
- Use deep ocean blue, ink violet, blue-gray, night green, or source dark tones as a non-black base. Shift 3–5 source colors toward adjacent luminous night hues. High saturation occupies only small areas. White is only a soft transition or the single main luminous core.
- Preserve 35–50% quiet dark or low-density gradient space, with subtle hue and value drift rather than black holes.
- Foreground membranes, middle color haze, and dim background light may overlap, but must read as large color fields rather than clouds, nebulae, smoke, petal rain, or literal sky.
- Use one broad, soft, low-contrast Bloom at the main color core only; never glowing object outlines. Nearly invisible uniform dithering may prevent banding. No coarse grain, paper, pigment sediment, chalk, or dirty spray texture.

## Clean luminous softness

Keep a soft, flowing, translucent dream quality. Cleanliness means absence of artifacts, not hard geometry, glass UI, product rendering, or mechanical optics. Make exposure approximately one stop brighter than a conventional deep-night image, with open colored shadows, ample midtones, and a translucent focal core that does not clip white.

Softness comes from broad color falloff, transparent overlaps, and low-contrast transitions, not smoke particles, noise, blur filters, or gray overlays. At 100%, no visible noise, coarse grain, fibers, grass-like luminous strokes, hair-like lines, dirt, or compression blocks. Do not erase everything into plastic. Allow barely perceptible low-frequency color flow; forbid dense high-frequency texture, sharp refraction lines, regular hard bands, and excessive sharpening.

## Embed identity anchors

Keep only 1–3 anchors as cropped contours, translucent silhouettes, low-detail structures, locally clear slices, or remnants swallowed by color. At least one anchor must be penetrated, obscured, or dissolved by 30–70%. One small clear region may carry recognition; other anchors must soften or abstract. Do not scatter clarity everywhere. Color fields need not obey literal light sources: do not generate the original scene at night. Preserve continuous soft mixing even when local lighting becomes impossible.

Reject and regenerate the lower panel if realism dominates, texture is clear everywhere, background becomes clouds/stars/nebula/smoke, there is only one spot or a linear two-color gradient, visible radial halos, neon outlines, bokeh, lens flare, large white glow, color merely surrounds objects, visible dirt/noise/fibers, glass UI, product geometry, or dead-black shadows.

## Subject adaptation

- People: preserve identity, facial proportions, and pose; transform clothing, shadows, hair, or environment. Do not invent a different face.
- Animals: preserve species, head, silhouette, and characteristic pose; translate fur, feathers, or motion into night color fields.
- Plants: preserve stems, leaf structure, flower centers, or growth direction; enlarge parts into translucent spaces, floating membranes, or temporal echoes.
- Landscapes: preserve horizon, terrain/water direction, and depth; reverse sky/ground or turn paths and water into luminous flow.
- Architecture/cities: preserve perspective, skyline, and identifying volumes; rearrange window rhythms, reflections, or facades into floating space.
- Objects/products/food: preserve outline and functional structure; transform material, shadow, liquid, or interior space. Do not invent brand text.
- Vehicles/machines: preserve identifying proportions and structures; use motion echoes, environmental color flow, or partial transparency, without arbitrary extra parts.
- Multiple subjects: preserve original relationships and count logic; echoes and abstraction must not imply extra real subjects.

## Required generation semantics

Include: same theme and recognizable identity anchors, newly staged composition; substantial source-derived surreal transformation, not a filtered repaint; gradient-first abstract hierarchy; 65–80% smooth fields and 20–35% simplified anchors; 3–5 asymmetrical overlapping freeform mesh-gradient fields on a non-black night base; an off-canvas field and one crossing/dissolving an anchor; visible mixed intermediate colors; broad transparent falloff and fully dissolved edges; brighter open colored shadows and generous midtones; clean soft haze without grain, dirt, fibers, hair-like detail, or smoke texture; one restrained broad bloom, quiet breathing space, soft digital/holographic materiality; no text, watermark, border, or unrelated fantasy objects.

## Production and quality control

1. Inspect the source, select a safe 16:9 top crop, and define theme, identity anchors, source colors, and surreal proposition.
2. Generate only the lower artwork, using the source as reference.
3. Check the thumbnail: 3–5 large fields and their overlap should register before a literal scene. Fully rendered realism appearing first fails.
4. Check field dominance, off-canvas entry, anchor penetration, intermediate colors, dissolved edges, one main Bloom, and quiet space.
5. Check unchanged theme, sufficient recognition, no unrelated objects, identity drift, or accidental added/missing subjects.
6. Inspect at 100% for luminous softness. Noise, fibers, dirty gray, blocked shadows, and hard product surfaces require a retry.
7. Check smooth gradients and preserved highlights. Do not rely on aggressive denoising or plastic/hard surfaces to simulate cleanliness.
8. Use [compose_9x16.py](../scripts/compose_9x16.py), or equivalent deterministic composition, to combine the actual 16:9 crop with the generated lower panel.
9. Confirm untouched evidence and exact 9:16 dimensions.

For a retry, do not merely request more blur or denoising. Preserve freeform fields and soft layering, remove terms causing grain/fibers/dirty fog, request brighter open shadows, clean soft haze, no visible grain or fibers, and no hard-edged glass UI; specify which field dissolves which anchor. If the theme disappears, restore only 1–3 locally clear anchors, not the whole original composition. Retry at most once unless the user asks for more.

Show the final 9:16 artwork and one short sentence about the crop anchor, surreal proposition, and night gradient fields. Do not expose the full internal prompt by default.
